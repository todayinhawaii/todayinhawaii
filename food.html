Hawaii Food Guide

From poke bowls to malasadas, plate lunches to shave ice — your complete guide to the most delicious food in Hawaii across all four islands.

🍽️ 12 Must-Try Hawaii Foods

You haven't truly experienced Hawaii until you've tried these iconic local dishes. Don't leave without eating every single one!

🐟
Hawaii's Signature Dish
Poke Bowl

Hawaii's greatest gift to the world. Fresh raw fish (usually ahi tuna) marinated in soy sauce, sesame oil and sea salt, served over rice with toppings like avocado, edamame and crispy onions. Every island has its own style.

📍 Where to find it: Every grocery store, fish market and poke shop across all islands
💰 Price: $10–$18
💡 Pro Tip: Foodland supermarket has some of the best poke on each island — don't overlook the grocery store! Ask for shoyu ahi, spicy ahi or limu poke.
🍱
Local Comfort Food
Plate Lunch

The ultimate local Hawaii meal. A scoop of white rice, a scoop of macaroni salad and your choice of protein — teriyaki chicken, kalua pork, loco moco, katsu chicken or beef stew. Pure comfort food and quintessentially Hawaiian.

📍 Where to find it: Local diners, lunch wagons and mom-and-pop restaurants island-wide
💰 Price: $9–$16
💡 Pro Tip: Look for lunch wagons (food trucks) — they often serve the most authentic and delicious plate lunches at the best prices.
🍧
Must Try Sweet Treat
Shave Ice

Not a snow cone — shave ice is an art form in Hawaii. Ultra-fine ice shaved to a silky smooth texture and drenched in tropical flavored syrups. The best versions include a scoop of ice cream at the bottom and sweetened azuki beans.

📍 Where to find it: Haleiwa on Oahu, Ululani's on Maui, multiple spots on every island
💰 Price: $4–$8
💡 Pro Tip: Always say yes to ice cream at the bottom and mochi balls on top. Rainbow syrup (strawberry, lemon, blue vanilla) is the classic combination.
🍩
Portuguese-Hawaiian Tradition
Malasadas

Portuguese fried doughnuts brought to Hawaii by immigrant sugarcane workers. Light, airy, deep-fried and rolled in sugar — they are absolutely irresistible fresh and hot from the fryer. Leonard's Bakery on Oahu is legendary.

📍 Where to find it: Leonard's Bakery (Oahu), Tex Drive-In (Big Island), Champion Malasadas (Oahu)
💰 Price: $1.50–$3 each
💡 Pro Tip: Get them fresh and hot — they don't keep well. Try the filled versions with haupia (coconut) or dobash (chocolate) cream inside.
🍳
Born in Hawaii 1949
Loco Moco

A uniquely Hawaiian invention — white rice topped with a hamburger patty, a fried egg and smothered in rich brown gravy. Pure comfort food perfection. Some places add Portuguese sausage, spam or kalua pork for island twists.

📍 Where to find it: Diners and local restaurants across all islands — especially good at Cafe 100 in Hilo
💰 Price: $10–$16
💡 Pro Tip: Cafe 100 in Hilo, Big Island claims to have invented loco moco and has over 30 variations. A pilgrimage for foodies!
🍱
Hawaii's Favorite Snack
Spam Musubi

Don't laugh — this is one of Hawaii's most beloved snack foods. Grilled spam on a block of seasoned rice wrapped in nori seaweed. A remnant of WWII military rations that Hawaii turned into a culinary icon.

📍 Where to find it: 7-Eleven, ABC Stores, gas stations and convenience stores island-wide
💰 Price: $2–$4
💡 Pro Tip: The best spam musubi are found warm at mom-and-pop shops and local supermarkets in the morning. Try the teriyaki spam version.
🐷
Traditional Hawaiian
Kalua Pig

A whole pig wrapped in ti leaves and slow-cooked underground in an imu (earth oven) for hours. The result is impossibly tender, smoky, pull-apart pork with a deeply Hawaiian flavor. The centerpiece of every traditional luau.

📍 Where to find it: Traditional luaus, local plate lunch spots and Helena's Hawaiian Food on Oahu
💰 Price: $12–$20 plate
💡 Pro Tip: Helena's Hawaiian Food in Honolulu (James Beard Award winner) serves some of the most authentic kalua pig outside of a luau.
🍜
Hawaii's Soul Food
Saimin

Hawaii's version of noodle soup — thin wheat noodles in a savory dashi broth topped with char siu pork, fishcake, green onions and a soft boiled egg. A beloved local comfort food unique to Hawaii, born from Japanese, Chinese and Filipino culinary traditions.

📍 Where to find it: Hamura Saimin in Lihue (Kauai), local diners across all islands, even McDonald's in Hawaii!
💰 Price: $8–$14
💡 Pro Tip: Hamura Saimin in Lihue, Kauai is a pilgrimage for locals — this tiny no-frills spot has been serving legendary saimin since 1952.
🐟
Traditional Hawaiian
Lomi Lomi Salmon

A traditional Hawaiian dish of salted salmon massaged (lomi means massage in Hawaiian) with tomatoes, onions and green onions. The bright, fresh flavors are the perfect counterpoint to rich kalua pig at a luau plate.

📍 Where to find it: Traditional luaus, Helena's Hawaiian Food, local Hawaiian restaurants
💰 Price: Usually part of a Hawaiian plate $14–$22
💡 Pro Tip: This dish is best experienced as part of a full traditional Hawaiian plate alongside poi, kalua pig and haupia. Try it at a real luau.
🍮
Traditional Hawaiian Dessert
Haupia

A traditional Hawaiian coconut pudding dessert with a firm, silky texture somewhere between jello and panna cotta. Pure coconut flavor, pure white color and completely addictive. Often served at luaus and as a pie filling.

📍 Where to find it: Luaus, local bakeries, Village Burger on Maui, Ted's Bakery on Oahu
💰 Price: $3–$6 slice
💡 Pro Tip: Ted's Bakery on Oahu's North Shore makes a legendary chocolate haupia cream pie that is worth the drive to the North Shore alone.
🌮
Fresh & Local
Fresh Fish Tacos

With the freshest fish in the world caught daily just offshore, Hawaii's fish tacos are in a league of their own. Mahi-mahi, ono (wahoo), ahi and opah wrapped in warm tortillas with tropical slaw and fresh salsa.

📍 Where to find it: Fish markets, beach shacks and casual restaurants across all islands
💰 Price: $12–$18
💡 Pro Tip: Ask what fish was caught TODAY — the best spots change their menu based on the morning's catch. Ono (wahoo) fish tacos are extraordinary.
🫐
Hawaii Made Famous
Acai Bowl

Hawaii helped popularize the acai bowl to the world. Thick blended frozen acai topped with granola, fresh tropical fruits, honey and coconut flakes. Every island has its own favorite toppings — lilikoi (passion fruit) is uniquely Hawaiian.

📍 Where to find it: Juice bars, health cafes and surf shops across all islands
💰 Price: $10–$15
💡 Pro Tip: Add lilikoi (passion fruit) to any acai bowl for the most Hawaiian experience. Surfer-owned shops usually make the best ones.
